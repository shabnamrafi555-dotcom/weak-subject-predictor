Stage 1

Problem scoping

Who

Class 10 students preparing for board exams, and their parents and teachers.

What

Students do not know which subjects need the most time until results arrive.

Where

At home while planning revision, at any time in the school year.

Why

Finding weak subjects early lets students spend limited hours where they matter most.

Problem statement: How might we help Class 10 students see which subjects are at risk, so they can plan revision time wisely?

Stage 2

Data acquisition

Each record is one student in one subject, with five inputs and one result:

  • Last exam mark (%)
  • Study hours per week for that subject
  • Attendance (%)
  • Homework completed (%)
  • Confidence in the subject (1 to 5)
  • Target: weak (next exam below 55%) or not weak

Honest note: this demo dataset has 600 synthetic records generated from realistic patterns (more study, attendance and homework lead to better marks, plus random variation). For the final submission, collect real data with a class survey and replace it. The site retrains itself on the data in the script.

Stage 4

Modelling

Model: logistic regression. It gives each input a weight, adds them up, and turns the total into a risk between 0% and 100%. It was chosen because it answers a yes/no question, is fast in a browser, and its weights are easy to explain. The model trains on 80% of the data when this page loads.

What the model learned (bigger bar = bigger effect on risk)

Bars pointing to "raises risk" mean a higher value makes a subject more likely to be weak. Bars pointing to "lowers risk" mean a higher value protects you.

Stage 5

Evaluation

The model was tested on the 20% of records it never saw during training.

Precision: when it says "weak", how often is it right. Recall: of all truly weak subjects, how many it caught. Recall matters most here, because missing a weak subject is worse than a false alarm.

Stage 6

Deployment

The predictor at the top of this page is the deployed product. It runs completely in your browser, so no marks are sent or stored anywhere.

  • Works offline as a single file, on phone and computer.
  • Use "Save as PDF" after a prediction to keep or share your plan.
  • Limits: it is a guide, not a guarantee. It does not know exam difficulty or personal situations. Talk to your teachers too.
  • Next steps: train on real class survey data, add more subjects, and track improvement each term.
